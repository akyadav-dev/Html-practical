About Taj Mahal

The Taj Mahal is a 17th-century white marble mausoleum in Agra, India, built by Mughal Emperor Shah Jahan in memory of his wife Mumtaz Mahal. Historical Background The Taj Mahal was commissioned in 1631 by Shah Jahan after the death of his beloved wife Mumtaz Mahal, who died during childbirth of their 14th child. Construction began in 1632 and the main mausoleum was completed in 1648, while the surrounding buildings, gardens, and gateways were finished by 1653. The total cost at the time was estimated at ₹32 million, equivalent to approximately US$827 million today. Shah Jahan himself is also buried beside Mumtaz Mahal in the tomb complex.

Location and Layout Situated on the right bank of the Yamuna River in Agra, Uttar Pradesh, the Taj Mahal complex spans about 17 hectares (42 acres). It includes the central mausoleum, a mosque, a guest house, formal gardens, reflecting pools, and subsidiary tombs. The complex is enclosed by a crenellated wall on three sides, with the main entrance on the south.

Architecture and Design The Taj Mahal is considered the pinnacle of Mughal architecture, blending Persian, Islamic, and Indian styles. The central structure is made of white Makrana marble, adorned with intricate pietra dura inlays of semi-precious stones such as jasper, jade, and turquoise. Its symmetrical design features a large central dome, four minarets, and detailed calligraphy of Quranic verses. The gardens follow the charbagh layout, symbolizing paradise, with water channels and fountains enhancing the aesthetic appeal.

Cultural Significance The Taj Mahal is widely regarded as a symbol of eternal love and is one of the most iconic monuments in the world. It was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983 and was named one of the New Seven Wonders of the World in 2007. Millions of tourists visit annually to admire its beauty and historical importance.

Preservation Over time, the Taj Mahal has faced environmental challenges, including discoloration due to pollution and acid rain. Conservation efforts by the Archaeological Survey of India aim to preserve its marble and intricate inlay work for future generations

Legacy The Taj Mahal remains a masterpiece of architectural innovation and artistic expression, celebrated for its harmonious proportions, exquisite craftsmanship, and emotional significance. It continues to inspire poets, historians, and travelers worldwide, embodying the enduring legacy of Mughal art and culture
